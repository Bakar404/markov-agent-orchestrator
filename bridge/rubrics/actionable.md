Judge the two answers on whether they would actually help an engineer fix the problem.

1. **Specificity** — does it name concrete mechanisms, files, commands or thresholds, or does it
   stay at the level of "improve caching" and "add monitoring"? Generic advice scores low even
   when it is correct.
2. **Constraint satisfaction** — the task states constraints. An answer that violates one, or
   quietly ignores it, loses to an answer that respects it even if the latter is less ambitious.
3. **Honesty about uncertainty** — does it distinguish what it verified from what it assumed?
   An answer that flags an unknown beats one that papers over it with confident prose.
4. **Coherence** — does the reasoning follow, and do the steps connect to the diagnosis?

Length is not quality. Confidence is not quality. Prefer the answer a competent engineer could
act on tomorrow morning.
